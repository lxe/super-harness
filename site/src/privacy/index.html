Privacy

Updated November 26, 2026

Super Harness has no accounts, analytics, advertising or tracking. The apps run on your own devices and talk to each other. This page describes the one service we run that your data passes through, the push notification relay, and what this website records.

The desktop app

The desktop app reads the coding harnesses installed on your computer (such as Codex, Claude, Antigravity, Muse and Pi) and shows their sessions in one interface. Your transcripts, prompts and files stay where those harnesses keep them. Super Harness does not upload them to us, and we have no server that receives them.

Each harness talks to its own provider when you use it. What a provider does with your prompts and code is governed by that provider's terms and privacy policy.

The iOS app

  • It talks to your desktop. After pairing, the iOS app connects only to the Super Harness desktop app you paired it with, over your local network or Tailscale. Sessions, transcripts, the messages you send, attachments and settings travel between your phone and your desktop.
  • Pairing. Pairing gives the phone a token for your desktop. The token and the desktop's address are stored in the iOS Keychain on your phone. Unpairing, in the app or on the desktop, makes the token stop working.
  • Camera. The camera is used only to scan the pairing QR code shown on your desktop. No photo or video is saved or sent.
  • Local network. The app looks for Super Harness desktops on your local network so you can pick one to pair with.
  • Microphone. When you dictate a message, the recording goes to your own desktop, which transcribes it with the dictation server you configured there. We never receive it.
  • Photos and files. Photos and files you attach are sent to your desktop for the thread you attach them to.
  • Demo mode. The demo shows sample chats that are built into the app and sends nothing anywhere.

The push notification relay

Notifications reach an iPhone through Apple's push notification service, which requires an Apple key that we hold. So that your desktop does not need that key, it sends notifications through a small relay we run on Cloudflare Workers. The relay is the only server of ours that the apps contact.

  • What your phone registers. When notifications are on and the phone is paired, the iOS app sends the relay its Apple push token, whether the build uses Apple's production or development service, and a relay key with its identifier. The key is derived from your pairing token by a one-way hash, so the relay cannot recover the pairing token or reach your desktop with it. No name, email address or account is involved.
  • What the relay keeps. One record per pairing: the relay key and up to four push tokens with the time each was last updated. A record is deleted automatically 45 days after it was last updated.
  • What passes through. When a task finishes or an agent needs input, your desktop sends the relay the notification's title and text, the kind of notification, and the session and harness it concerns. The relay passes these to Apple for delivery and does not store them. Silent refresh notifications carry no text.
  • Who else sees it. Apple delivers the notification under its own privacy policy. Cloudflare runs the relay and, as with any website, handles the IP addresses of requests to it.

The app contacts the relay only when it is paired and you have allowed notifications. If you turn notifications off in the app, your desktop stops sending them, and the relay's record expires 45 days after its last update.

This website

superharness.tech is a set of static pages. It sets no cookies, stores nothing in your browser, and loads no analytics, ad trackers or third-party scripts. The web server logs each request (IP address, time, the file asked for, the referring page and the browser) to keep the site running, and the logs are deleted after two weeks.

Children

Super Harness is a tool for software developers and is not directed at children.

Questions and changes

To ask about this policy, open an issue on GitHub. If the policy changes, the new version appears here with a new date.
